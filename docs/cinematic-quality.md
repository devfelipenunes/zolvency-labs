# Cinematic quality studies

- Wall match cut: opt-in `?cut=wall` uses matched opaque surface approaches in decoded server/corridor geometry, an explicit narrow solid occlusion mask during the camera splice, and deterministic progress in both directions. The mask is an editorial fallback, not evidence that the wall covers every pixel. If no matching geometry is found the original rail remains. Test actual sampled camera clearance and capture all nine frames before approval.
- Suspended quantum: current Grab3D GLB has one mesh body; the whole model includes the original stand and cannot be plausibly lifted as-is. Inspect the actual hangar overhead geometry and test the upper room clearance. Bluefors room-temperature flange: https://ldsl-system.bluefors.com/room-temperature-flange ; IBM chandelier: https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/modular-cryogenics . Alternative external CC0 source candidate: https://blendswap.com/blend/31865 (Blender source; verify file, hierarchy, geometry, provenance and export). Do not claim installation or physical structural qualification from a raycast.
- Studio: opt-in `?quality=studio` preserves original PBR material colors, applies four native area lights, higher environment intensity, controlled exposure and texture anisotropy. Existing cinema mode is the separate HDRI/GTAO/bloom comparison. Area lights themselves do not cast shadows. No physical GPU benchmark yet.
- Next: audited secondary UVs and Cycles indirect light bake per room; actual CC0 metal surface maps only when UV layout is understood (https://polyhaven.com/a/galvanized_sheet_metal and https://polyhaven.com/a/blue_metal_plate); avoid weathering the clean lab. See https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/render/cycles/baking.html and https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html .
- Release gates: compare real captures, camera clearance, frame time on physical desktop/mobile devices, and texture bytes. Keep default mode unchanged until acceptance.
